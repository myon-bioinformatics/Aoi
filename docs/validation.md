# Aoi Validation Framework

This document defines how Aoi evaluates evidence, tests hypotheses, challenges explanations, and updates understanding.

Validation is one of the core activities of the Aoi ecosystem.

The purpose of validation is not to protect conclusions.

The purpose of validation is to determine whether explanations remain useful when confronted with evidence.

---

# Why Validation Matters

Within Aoi, a hypothesis is never considered complete merely because it sounds reasonable.

Explanations must interact with evidence.

A good explanation survives investigation.

A poor explanation fails investigation.

Both outcomes are valuable.

Without validation:

- observations become stories
- models become beliefs
- assumptions become invisible

Validation exists to prevent this.

---

# Validation Is Not Confirmation

Aoi does not validate hypotheses by searching only for supporting evidence.

Aoi validates hypotheses by exposing them to evidence that could potentially weaken them.

The objective is not confirmation.

The objective is evaluation.

Validation should be capable of producing:

- support
- modification
- refinement
- rejection

All outcomes are acceptable.

---

# Evidence First

Evidence always takes priority over preference.

Evidence always takes priority over elegance.

Evidence always takes priority over intuition.

If a preferred explanation conflicts with the evidence:

investigate the explanation.

Do not modify the evidence to preserve the explanation.

---

# What Validation Attempts To Answer

Validation attempts to answer questions such as:

- Does the hypothesis explain the observation?
- Does the hypothesis explain multiple observations?
- Does the hypothesis survive new data?
- Does the hypothesis outperform simpler explanations?
- Does the hypothesis remain useful over time?

Validation is therefore comparative rather than absolute.

---

# Sources Of Validation

Aoi may validate hypotheses using many forms of evidence.

Examples include:

- historical observations
- newly acquired datasets
- residual analysis
- statistical testing
- model comparison
- counterexamples
- long-term trends

No single validation method is universally sufficient.

---

# Validation Through Residuals

Residuals play a special role within Aoi.

A hypothesis should reduce unexplained observations.

If large residuals remain:

further investigation may be required.

Residuals do not automatically invalidate a hypothesis.

However, residuals should always be documented.

---

# Validation Through Counterexamples

Counterexamples are among the strongest validation tools.

When an explanation claims:

> A causes B

A counterexample asks:

> Does B always occur when A exists?

or

> Can B occur without A?

Counterexamples help identify boundaries.

Hypotheses should survive challenges.

Not avoid them.

---

# Validation Through Time

Aoi places significant value on persistence.

Single observations are useful.

Long-term observations are often more informative.

Validation should therefore consider:

- short-term behavior
- medium-term behavior
- long-term behavior

A hypothesis that survives multiple years deserves greater confidence than one supported by a single season.

---

# Validation Through Comparison

A hypothesis should not be evaluated in isolation.

Alternative explanations should be considered whenever possible.

For example:

Hypothesis A:

> Run differential explains team performance.

Hypothesis B:

> One-run game performance explains team performance.

Hypothesis C:

> Bullpen management explains team performance.

Validation should examine which explanation:

- explains more observations
- generates fewer contradictions
- produces fewer residuals

The objective is not to find perfection.

The objective is to find improvement.

---

# Falsification

Aoi explicitly accepts falsification.

If evidence weakens a hypothesis:

update it.

If evidence rejects a hypothesis:

replace it.

If evidence supports a competing explanation:

consider the alternative.

The ability to be wrong is a requirement for usefulness.

An explanation that cannot be challenged cannot be validated.

---

# Validation Outcomes

Validation does not produce only two outcomes.

A hypothesis may become:

## Supported

Current evidence remains consistent.

---

## Refined

Part of the hypothesis survives.

Part requires modification.

---

## Split

Multiple explanations emerge.

Further investigation becomes necessary.

---

## Inconclusive

Available evidence remains insufficient.

The question stays open.

---

## Rejected

Evidence no longer supports the explanation.

The hypothesis should be archived.

---

# Validation Records

Validation results should be preserved.

Future researchers should be able to answer:

- What hypothesis was tested?
- What evidence was used?
- What assumptions existed?
- What result was obtained?

Reproducibility is essential.

Validation history is part of the research record.

---

# PythDRagoras And Validation

PythDRagoras serves as the validation engine of the Aoi ecosystem.

Its role is not prediction.

Its role is not reporting.

Its role is to challenge explanations.

PythDRagoras asks:

- What remains unexplained?
- What assumption is hidden?
- What evidence disagrees?
- What alternative explanation exists?

Validation is the mechanism through which those questions are explored.

---

# The Role Of Uncertainty

Validation should reduce uncertainty when possible.

However, uncertainty should not be hidden.

If uncertainty remains:

document it.

If evidence is insufficient:

document it.

If multiple explanations remain plausible:

document them.

Aoi prefers visible uncertainty over false certainty.

---

# Continuous Validation

Validation is not a one-time activity.

New observations may appear.

New datasets may emerge.

New explanations may become available.

Validation should therefore be considered continuous.

Every conclusion remains provisional.

Every explanation remains revisable.

Every model remains incomplete.

---

# Final Principle

Validation is not the defense of an explanation.

Validation is the process by which explanations earn confidence.

A hypothesis becomes useful by surviving evidence.

An explanation becomes stronger by surviving challenge.

Aoi therefore validates not to protect ideas.

Aoi validates to improve them.
