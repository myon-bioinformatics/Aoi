# Aoi Hypothesis Framework

This document defines how hypotheses are generated, evaluated, preserved, and evolved within the Aoi ecosystem.

Aoi considers hypotheses to be first-class research artifacts.

They are not intermediate steps.

They are not disposable notes.

They are not unfinished conclusions.

Hypotheses are among the primary outputs of the system.

---

# Why Hypotheses Matter

Most analytical systems are designed to generate answers.

Aoi is designed to generate understanding.

Understanding often begins with questions.

Questions lead to hypotheses.

Hypotheses lead to investigation.

Investigation leads to evidence.

Evidence leads to revised understanding.

Because of this cycle, hypotheses are treated as assets rather than temporary artifacts.

---

# What Is A Hypothesis?

Within Aoi, a hypothesis is a proposed explanation that has not yet been sufficiently validated.

A hypothesis is not:

- a fact
- a conclusion
- a belief

A hypothesis is:

- testable
- revisable
- falsifiable
- observable

A good hypothesis creates opportunities for investigation.

---

# The Origin Of Aoi's Hypothesis Thinking

Aoi began with an observation that did not feel fully explained.

The initial question was:

> Why does this team keep losing?

Over time the question evolved:

> Why do existing metrics fail to explain the result?

Eventually another question appeared:

> What assumptions are hidden inside the explanation?

The project discovered that useful hypotheses often emerge from unexplained observations.

This remains the foundation of the framework.

---

# Sources Of Hypotheses

Aoi expects hypotheses to emerge from multiple sources.

---

## Residuals

A difference between:

- prediction and reality
- expected outcome and observed outcome

may indicate missing explanations.

Residuals are among the highest-priority hypothesis generators.

---

## Contradictions

When two observations appear incompatible:

a hypothesis may explain the conflict.

Contradictions are valuable.

---

## Counterexamples

A single counterexample can reveal the boundaries of an explanation.

Counterexamples frequently generate high-quality hypotheses.

---

## Unknown Observations

Data that cannot be classified should not be discarded.

Unknown observations frequently indicate missing categories.

Missing categories often lead to new hypotheses.

---

## Long-Term Patterns

Repeated observations across multiple years may reveal structure that is invisible within a single season.

Aoi places significant value on persistent observations.

---

# Hypothesis Lifecycle

Aoi treats hypotheses as evolving entities.

```text
Observation
    ↓

Question
    ↓

Hypothesis
    ↓

Investigation
    ↓

Evidence
    ↓

Validation

    ├── Supported
    ├── Refined
    ├── Rejected
    └── Split
```

No hypothesis is considered permanent.

Every hypothesis remains revisable.

---

# Hypothesis Quality

A strong hypothesis should be:

## Observable

Something in reality should be measurable.

---

## Testable

A process should exist that could evaluate the claim.

---

## Falsifiable

Evidence should be capable of disproving it.

---

## Specific

Broad explanations are difficult to investigate.

Specificity is preferred.

---

## Reproducible

Different observers should be able to reproduce the investigation.

---

# Examples

Weak hypothesis:

> The team loses because it is weak.

Difficult to test.

Difficult to measure.

Difficult to falsify.

---

Stronger hypothesis:

> Teams with a significantly negative one-run-game record will underperform their expected win percentage.

This is measurable.

Observable.

Testable.

Potentially falsifiable.

---

# Competing Hypotheses

Aoi does not require a single hypothesis.

Multiple competing hypotheses may coexist.

Examples:

Hypothesis A:

> Run differential explains the outcome.

Hypothesis B:

> One-run game performance explains the outcome.

Hypothesis C:

> Bullpen usage explains the outcome.

These hypotheses should compete through evidence rather than preference.

---

# Rejected Hypotheses

Rejected hypotheses are valuable.

A rejected hypothesis provides information.

It reveals:

- failed assumptions
- incorrect reasoning
- model boundaries

Rejected hypotheses should be preserved whenever practical.

The history of investigation matters.

---

# PythDRagoras And Hypotheses

PythDRagoras acts as the hypothesis engine of the ecosystem.

Its responsibility is not primarily prediction.

Its responsibility is discovery.

PythDRagoras searches for:

- residuals
- contradictions
- unexplained outcomes
- assumption boundaries

and transforms them into candidate hypotheses.

Questions are treated as legitimate outputs.

Uncertainty is treated as useful information.

---

# Principle Of Continuous Revision

A hypothesis should never become protected from evidence.

Evidence always has priority.

If evidence weakens a hypothesis:

revise it.

If evidence rejects a hypothesis:

replace it.

If evidence creates a better hypothesis:

follow it.

Aoi favors adaptation over consistency.

---

# Final Principle

A hypothesis is not a destination.

A hypothesis is a bridge between observation and understanding.

The purpose of Aoi is not to manufacture certainty.

The purpose of Aoi is to continuously improve explanations.

Every explanation begins as a hypothesis.

Every hypothesis begins as a question.

Every question begins with an observation.
