# Aoi Research Method

This document defines the standard research workflow used throughout the Aoi ecosystem.

The purpose of this workflow is not to maximize prediction accuracy.

The purpose is to maximize understanding.

Aoi is fundamentally a research-oriented system.

Research therefore takes priority over implementation convenience.

---

# Overview

The Aoi research process follows a continuous cycle.

```text
Observation
    ↓

Documentation
    ↓

Question
    ↓

Hypothesis
    ↓

Analysis
    ↓

Validation
    ↓

Revision
    ↓

New Observation
```

The cycle intentionally never ends.

The purpose is continuous refinement.

Not permanent conclusions.

---

# Stage 1: Observation

Every investigation begins with observation.

Observation must be separated from interpretation.

Examples:

Observation:

- A team finished fifth.
- A model failed to predict an outcome.
- A residual increased.
- A contradiction appeared.

These are observations.

No explanation should be attached yet.

The objective is simply to preserve reality.

---

# Stage 2: Documentation

Observations should be preserved before interpretation begins.

Questions to ask:

- What was observed?
- When was it observed?
- What evidence exists?
- Can the observation be reproduced?

The objective is to make future investigation possible.

Poor documentation destroys future understanding.

---

# Stage 3: Question Formation

Aoi considers questions to be valuable outputs.

A good question should emerge from observation.

Examples:

Weak:

> Why is Team A bad?

Stronger:

> Why does Team A consistently underperform relative to its expected performance?

Even stronger:

> Which assumptions fail when expected performance diverges from observed performance?

The objective is not certainty.

The objective is curiosity.

---

# Stage 4: Hypothesis Generation

Questions lead to hypotheses.

Hypotheses should remain:

- observable
- testable
- falsifiable
- reproducible

Examples:

Hypothesis:

> One-run game performance contributes significantly to long-term underperformance.

Hypothesis:

> Existing metrics systematically miss bullpen-related effects.

Hypothesis:

> A hidden variable exists between run differential and final standings.

Every hypothesis should aim to explain an observation.

---

# Stage 5: Analysis

Analysis attempts to measure the hypothesis.

Examples:

- descriptive statistics
- distributions
- residual analysis
- comparative analysis
- historical analysis
- simulations

Analysis is not validation.

Analysis produces evidence.

Validation evaluates evidence.

The distinction matters.

---

# Stage 6: Validation

Validation challenges explanations.

The objective is not confirmation.

The objective is stress testing.

Questions include:

- Does the hypothesis survive new data?
- Does it survive multiple seasons?
- Does it survive counterexamples?
- Does it produce fewer residuals?

A hypothesis that survives challenge earns confidence.

---

# Stage 7: Revision

No hypothesis is final.

Validation may result in:

- refinement
- replacement
- expansion
- rejection

Revision is expected.

Revision is healthy.

Revision is evidence that learning occurred.

---

# Observation Hierarchy

Not all observations are equal.

Aoi generally values:

```text
Persistent Pattern
    >
Repeating Observation
    >
Single Observation
```

Long-term observations receive additional attention.

Persistence often reveals structure.

---

# Research Artifacts

Aoi treats the following as first-class artifacts:

- observations
- datasets
- hypotheses
- residuals
- counterexamples
- validations
- conclusions
- unanswered questions

A project is successful even if it produces better questions than answers.

---

# Residual-Driven Research

Residuals are one of the primary engines of discovery.

Whenever:

Reality
≠
Explanation

A residual exists.

The larger the residual becomes, the more valuable investigation becomes.

Residuals may indicate:

- missing variables
- hidden assumptions
- model boundaries
- measurement limitations

Residuals should be documented and preserved.

---

# Counterexample-Driven Research

Counterexamples deserve special attention.

A strong explanation should survive unusual cases.

When a counterexample appears:

Do not discard it.

Do not hide it.

Investigate it.

Counterexamples frequently reveal where understanding stops.

---

# Explanation Maturity Levels

Aoi recognizes different levels of confidence.

Level 0

Observation Only

No explanation exists.

---

Level 1

Initial Hypothesis

A possible explanation exists.

---

Level 2

Validated Hypothesis

The explanation survives evidence.

---

Level 3

Working Model

The explanation becomes practically useful.

---

Level 4

Established Framework

The explanation repeatedly survives challenge.

Even at this level:

the framework remains revisable.

# Research Success

A research effort should not be judged solely by predictive performance.

Research is successful when it improves understanding.

Success may include:

- discovering a new pattern
- identifying a hidden assumption
- reducing uncertainty
- improving a framework
- generating a better question

Not all research ends with a single answer.

---

# Final Principle

Research begins with observation.

Understanding begins with questions.

Questions create hypotheses.

Hypotheses generate evidence.

Evidence refines understanding.

Understanding generates new questions.

The cycle continues.

Aoi exists to support that cycle.
